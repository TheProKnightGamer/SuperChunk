# GPU-AHEAD PLAN — Compact-Readback Architecture (2026-07-01)

Mission: push the GPU ahead of the CPU. User-accepted relaxed parity (~'one or two blocks can flip').
Produced by the 4-lens research workflow (architecture / Amdahl / dead-end mechanics / prior art) + adversarial synthesis.

## Verdict

GO — build COMPACT-READBACK on the existing temporal K<=32 batcher + single-drainer topology, but gated behind four cheap discriminators (S/S/M/M) that can each kill or redirect it before the only L-cost build. Rationale: (1) it is the first architecture whose Amdahl ceiling (+20-45% gross, +15-30% honest) exceeds the worker-count lever; (2) it structurally avoids all five measured residency killers (per-chunk round-trips, coalescing collapse, launch explosion, 5-10MB blocking readback, short hide window) — 96KB/chunk ids ride the SAME batch chain already proven 1.000x-neutral at 78KB/chunk; (3) c2me-ocl ships this exact architecture in production (1B/block ids, bit7 fluid-tick flag, GPU aquifer, single queue per device, 1200-2500+ cps claims) — the pattern is commercially validated, though its code is ARR and must be clean-roomed. The two honest unknowns are the realized self-consistent flip rate (Stage 3) and the plumbing-tax recurrence (Stage 5); both are measured before the big build. NEW code-fact from this synthesis: FP_CONTRACT OFF is ALREADY applied on the emitted spline path (noise.cl:39 prepended to every fused program; aquifer.cl:33) yet the ~3e-8 drift persists — so Lens 4's source-fix is likely spent, drift-shrinking is worthless against the <1e-12 tie point-mass (flat to 1e-6), and only exact-zero drift or the measured flip census can clear the parity gate.

## Ceiling

If every gate GOes: +15-30% over today's tuned baseline = ~520-650 cps @12 workers (from 450-500) and ~730-860 cps @21 workers (from 632-664). Basis: Lens 2's net replaceable fraction f=0.22-0.32 (the only accounting that subtracts already-shipped lever harvest and double-counting) gives a defensible Amdahl ceiling of +20-45%, discounted for plumbing <=3pp, ~0.8% beard/blend fallback chunks, carver-stage aquifer share, and the new 0.5-2ms/chunk consume cost. Lens 1's 1.7x (~760-845 @12w) over-counts by ignoring shipped levers; treat as unreachable. Long-run architecture ceiling if c2me-class follow-ons also land (biome quart-id readback, surface-height area tiles, native bulk palette writes, spatial 4x4 tiling): ~1200-2000 cps on this i7-14700K + RTX 3070 per the c2me-ocl hardware-tier prior — NOT promised by this plan; the optional Stage 2 calibration run (c2me-ocl on a throwaway Fabric instance, zero code) bounds it empirically. Honest floor if plumbing recurs at half strength: ~+5-8%, i.e. barely above the mwc768 lever.

## Kill risks

- 1. REALIZED TIE-FLIP MAGNITUDE/CHARACTER (Stages 1+3 kill it early): the 1.16% tie population (~1,194 candidate blocks/chunk, all Y<=106) is a <1e-12 point-mass flat to 1e-6, so it flips under ANY nonzero drift and drift-SHRINKING cannot help — and FP_CONTRACT OFF is already active on this codebase (noise.cl:39, aquifer.cl:33) with the 3e-8 drift persisting, so Lens 4's source-fix is probably spent. If the self-consistent census lands at hundreds/chunk or spatially clustered (clamp-surface point masses look like features, not noise), 'one or two blocks can flip' does not cover it; the tie-band CPU assist fallback claws back several points of the win and adds a dual-path branch.
- 2. fp64 CORRUPTION ARTIFACT SURVIVES THE SINGLE-DRAINER TOPOLOGY (Stage 4): if E1 fails — i.e., the artifact is host-plumbing (H2) or marginal hardware (H4) rather than submission-concurrency — then 0.005-0.01% magnitude-unbounded, non-deterministic block corruption ships into SAVED terrain, which no relaxed-parity waiver covers. That parks aquifer-on-GPU, cuts the transferable CPU slice from ~25-32% to ~15-20%, and (if H4) haunts every future heavy-kernel design on this box. c2me's own 'deprioritize NVIDIA on Linux for stability' is avoidance, not a fix — there is no production precedent for our multi-queue fp64 failure mode.
- 3. PLUMBING-TAX RECURRENCE (Stage 5 tripwire before the L build): the residency decomposition proved -15.9pp of the -33% was pure integration plumbing, not bytes. Compact readback eliminates that specific seam by construction, but introduces its own (id lifecycle, GpuBatchStore churn, worker-side FluidStatus capture potentially shrinking achieved batch K, dual-path fallback branches). Every prior 'offload more' round on this box measured 0 or negative cps; if even half the plumbing class recurs (>8pp), the +15-30% collapses toward the ~+5% floor and the correct move is the spatial-tiling topology, not more patching.

# Research appendix (4 lenses)

## LENS 1 — NOISE-STAGE ARCHITECTURE (compact-readback feasibility, code-verified with file:line citations)

### Findings
- Q1 ALPHABET — The noise-stage fill loop produces a CLOSED 10-state alphabet in the overworld. doFill (decompiled NoiseBasedChunkGenerator.java:353-427, extracted to scratchpad) calls noisechunk.getInterpolatedState() = MaterialRuleList over exactly TWO rules built at NoiseChunk.java:148-158: (1) aquifer.computeSubstance(pos, cacheAllInCell(finalDensity + BeardifierMarker)) and (2) OreVeinifier iff oreVeinsEnabled. computeSubstance returns only {null->defaultBlock, AIR, WATER, LAVA} (Aquifer.java:163-273); OreVeinifier returns only {null, copper_ore, raw_copper_block, granite, deepslate_iron_ore, raw_iron_block, tuff} (OreVeinifier.java:22-62); null-from-all -> settings.defaultBlock()=STONE (NoiseBasedChunkGenerator.java:402-404, NoiseGeneratorSettings.java:122). AIR is never written (skipped, line 407). Total: air-skip + stone + water + lava + 6 vein states = 10 states = 4 bits; plus the per-block aquifer shouldScheduleFluidUpdate flag (1 bit) consumed at doFill lines 411-414 -> markPosForPostprocessing. One byte per block encodes everything with room to spare.
- Q1 — Deepslate, bedrock, and all surface materials are NOT noise-stage: deepslate is SurfaceRules.verticalGradient('deepslate', Y0..Y8) applied in the later buildSurface stage (SurfaceRuleData.java:307), bedrock likewise (SurfaceRuleData.java:297-302). Nether is even simpler: aquifersEnabled=false and oreVeinsEnabled=false (NoiseGeneratorSettings.java:103-117) -> alphabet {air, netherrack, lava-below-32} = 3 states via the trivial disabled-aquifer picker (Aquifer.java:43-56). End: {air, end_stone}.
- Q2 REMAINING CPU WORK — With a GPU byte-per-block array, the surviving per-block fill work is exactly: (a) map byte -> per-section palette id (today Noisium calls palette().idFor(blockState) PER BLOCK at NoiseChunkGeneratorMixin.java:47 — compact path does it once per state per section, <=10 entries) + BitStorage.set (NoiseChunkGeneratorMixin.java:48-51); (b) section counters nonEmptyBlockCount/tickingFluidCount/tickingBlockCount + Lithium flag counters (lines 26-57) — derivable from a per-section byte histogram; (c) two heightmaps OCEAN_FLOOR_WG + WORLD_SURFACE_WG (created at NoiseBasedChunkGenerator.java:355-356, updated per non-air block at 409-410) — with the known alphabet both reduce to a per-column top-down first-match scan (WORLD_SURFACE=first non-air; OCEAN_FLOOR=first motion-blocking, i.e. stone/ore ids, water+lava excluded); (d) markPosForPostprocessing for flagged fluid blocks (411-414, rare). EVERYTHING else in the loop dies: all interpolator updateForX/Y/Z lerps, MaterialRuleList dispatch, the whole aquifer per-block search/pressure path, vein DF evals + vein RNG, beardifier. No light-engine or aquifer-post-pass work exists inside doFill. Carvers reuse the CPU Aquifer object in a LATER stage (applyCarvers, NoiseBasedChunkGenerator.java:289) — that CPU path is untouched, so no coupling.
- Q2 MAGNITUDE — Removable JFR self-time (2026-06-29 profile, still current): DfcCompiled MulNode 11.95% + Aquifer refreshDistPosIdx 9.06% + computeSubstance 2.95% + MaterialRuleList.calculate 5.41% + NoiseChunk replaceIteration redirects ~5.2% + Beardifier ~4.9% = 39.5%, plus ~2-3pp of ImprovedNoise.sampleAndLerp 7.57% (aquifer barrier/floodedness noises) => ~40-42% of worker CPU is GPU-transferable by compact readback. PalettedContainer.get 11.9% (surface-rules/biome reads in later stages) and Climate RTree 3.6% remain. Amdahl ceiling at fixed workers: ~1.7x.
- Q3 BATCHER REUSE — The compact kernel slots into the EXISTING single-drainer batch chain with no new architecture: GpuBatchDispatcher.batchFill (GpuBatchDispatcher.java:232-312) runs one in-order queue: async origins upload -> df_batch_lattice_multichunk NDRange (line 292) -> blocking pinned readback (294-305). A chained decide kernel enqueued between lines 292 and 294 reads the K*M*1225 corner buffer DEVICE-RESIDENT (no intermediate readback; in-order queue = free ordering), writes K*98304 bytes, and the single blocking read shrinks from K*M*1225 doubles (~68.6KB/chunk) to K*96KB ids (+optionally keep corners for CPU fallback). Worker never blocks: the existing prefetch seam (GpuBatchPrefetch.wrapNoise, called from VanillaWorldGenerationDelegate:154 at the noise-status boundary) already releases the C2ME worker and deposits results in GpuBatchStore keyed by ChunkPos+geometry; the fill then consumes. This is precisely the architecture that made corner batching cost-neutral (coalescing ~715-917x), and it structurally avoids BOTH residency killers (per-chunk un-coalesced round-trips; begin->first-use window too short) AND the multi-worker fp64 corruption artifact (all fp64 dispatches serialize on the one drainer thread — the config the artifact never appeared in; live corner path is clean over 349.65M comparisons).
- Q3 EXTRA PER-CHUNK INPUTS — The exact upload contract ALREADY EXISTS as code: MixinAquiferSamplerImpl.scBuildAquiferAccumulator (MixinAquiferSamplerImpl.java:493-512) packages per-cell FluidStatus fluidLevel[]+fluidTypeId[] for all 315 aquifer cells (grid 3x35x3 for -64..320), the CPU-preloaded aquiferLocationCache (preloaded eagerly at aquifer <init>, lines 521-552 — or GPU-regenerable via the bit-exact Xoroshiro port in rng.cl), grid geometry (minGridX/Y/Z, gridSizeX/Z), and probed picker params (seaLevel, lavaLevel, defaultFluidId). aquifer_decide (aquifer.cl:209-241) consumes exactly these and is mode-A proven BUGS=0/29.85M. Only TWO inputs change vs the verify harness: density comes from on-device interpolation instead of CPU upload, and barrierNoise must be computed in-kernel (it is a plain DensityFunctions.noise, NoiseRouterData.java:340 — bread-and-butter for the emitter, which covers every AST node incl. splines, OpenCLAstEmitter.java:476-565). Vein decision needs only the 3 vein corner grids (already ride in the fused dispatch) + the oreRandom positional seed (Xoroshiro, proven bit-exact) + the per-block 3-draw nextFloat sequence (OreVeinifier.java:39-47, pure ported RNG). Per-cell computeFluid STAYS CPU (spline-bearing 48-step preliminarySurfaceLevel scan, NoiseChunk.java:200-212) but is per-CELL not per-block: ~35 amortized new cells/chunk with ScAquiferCellCache (default-on cross-chunk cache, MixinAquiferSamplerImpl.java:241) — computable at prefetch time when the NoiseChunk already exists.
- Q3 BEARDIFIER — Beard is added per BLOCK (not per corner): the blockStateRule density is cacheAllInCell(add(finalDensity, BeardifierMarker)) (NoiseChunk.java:149-151) and CacheAllInCell fills per-block values (NoiseChunk.java:506-535), with Beardifier.compute summing per-block over Rigid pieces + junctions (Beardifier.java:90-132). Uploading beard per block is a non-starter (786KB/chunk fp64 > the entire compact readback), but the GPU port is small and cheap: upload the static 13,824-float BEARD_KERNEL once (55KB, Beardifier.java:26-34) + per-chunk piece list (Rigid = 6-int box + adjustment + groundLevelDelta = ~32B; junction=16B). beardSkip's shipped verify proved 99.2% of blocks lie outside all beard AABBs, so the per-block piece loop is almost always zero-length; only Mth.fastInvSqrt (a deterministic double bit-trick) needs an exact port. Fallback option: chunks with a non-empty piece list route to the CPU fill path (a minority of chunks).
- Q4 INTERPOLATION ORDER — Both consumption orders already exist as bit-exact-at-fp64 kernels off the SAME device-resident corner buffer: df_full_field_interp (Y->X->Z updateForZ value path — what the vein DFs and aquifer-internal DF reads see) and df_full_field_interp_lerp3 (X->Y->Z Mth.lerp3 cell-cache path — what finalDensity-fed-to-aquifer sees) (fullfield.cl:42-93, 121-163; order rationale in headers 12-27 and 95-119). The decide kernel does NOT need to materialize full fields in global memory: it inlines the 8-corner fetch + mc_lerp/mc_lerp3 per work-item exactly as those kernels do, then applies finalDensity's small outside-the-interpolator tail (mul 0.64 -> squeeze -> min with noodle -> rangeChoice, NoiseRouterData.java:332-335,362 — all supported emitter nodes) + beard + aquifer/vein decision, and writes 1 byte. Under user-accepted relaxed parity even the order distinction is discretionary; keeping the matching orders costs nothing and minimizes drift.
- SELF-CONSISTENCY REFRAME of the spline gate — In compact-readback the SAME GPU density decides both solid-vs-open (density>0 -> stone) and the aquifer branch, so a boundary-tie that flips does so consistently for the block and its neighbors: the output is an internally consistent world that differs from vanilla at razor-edge tie blocks, not floating-water/seam corruption. The GPU value at (x,y,z) is independent of batch composition K (per-block math reads only that chunk's corners + per-chunk aux), so worlds stay run-to-run deterministic for a fixed seed. The mode-B '1.16%' population is boundary-PROXIMITY (blocks within 1e-12 of a decision boundary, count flat out to 1e-6 — a tie point-mass, all Y<=106), and 'up to ~half' flip by error sign — this is an upper bound measured under the CPU-density-vs-GPU-density MIXED regime; the actual self-consistent flip count/typology has never been measured and is the single go/no-go number.
- PRECEDENT ECONOMICS — the compact readback restores the exact transfer regime that is already proven neutral: 96KB/chunk ids ~= 1.2x today's ~78KB corner readback (which runs at 1.000x throughput cost through the same batcher), vs the 5.25-10.5MB/chunk residency readback that measured -33%. At the measured pinned 15.7GB/s, 96KB is ~6 microseconds/chunk of bus time. GPU-side ALU is a non-issue: the box is measured latency/launch-bound, not ALU-bound (fp32 vs fp64 throughput FLAT), the full-field interp kernel over the same 98304-point domain measured 0.36ms/chunk, and the added aquifer/vein/beard logic is tens of fp64 MFLOP/chunk against the 3070's ~316 GFLOP/s fp64.

### Risks
- THE tie-flip magnitude: ~1,194 boundary-tie blocks/chunk (Y<=106) with up-to-half flipping is potentially 2-3 orders of magnitude beyond the user's stated 'one or two blocks can flip' — mitigated by (a) it being an upper bound from the mixed regime, (b) self-consistency (flips move a razor-edge wall coherently rather than corrupting), but if actual flips land as isolated stone<->water toggles on aquifer walls they schedule fluid ticks and are visible. Must be measured, not argued.
- The mode-B tie mass being FLAT from 1e-12 to 1e-6 suggests exact-tie point masses (density exactly on a boundary), which could be spatially CLUSTERED (e.g. along clamp/saturation surfaces of the noodle-cave min() or slide regions) — clustered flips look like features, not noise.

- Prefetch-time aquifer input capture requires the aquifer/NoiseChunk to exist at wrapNoise time and 315-cell precompute adds latency to the prefetch leg; if it serializes before batching it could shrink achieved K (the lever that currently hits batchLimit=32).

### Risks
- The -15.9pp 'plumbing' tax is the loss class that survives byte-shrinking: compact-readback avoids the per-block mixin seam and the 12.6MB holder churn, but introduces its own (id-array lifecycle, dual-path fallback, store lookups). - computeFluid on GPU under relaxed parity changes FluidStatus at the CELL level: one flipped floodedness/level threshold moves a whole aquifer cell's water (or its surface Y by 1), which is categorically more visible than 1-2 block flips. This can exceed the user's tolerance even when per-block flip counts look fine — the verify must count cell-level divergences separately.
- The multi-worker fp64 concurrency artifact (~0.005-0.01% corrupted blocks, driver-level, per-thread isolation did NOT fix it) is unresolved. Single-drainer-queue dispatch is a plausible sidestep (all batched dispatches already come from one thread) and fp32 is the other — but block-ids are terrain-permanent, so silent corruption ships into saved chunks; a cheap always-on sanity check (e.g., per-batch id-histogram bounds or periodic 1-in-N chunk re-verify) should ship with it.
- Carver-stage aquifer use means the CPU Aquifer object (computeFluid path + cellCache) must remain fully functional — the aquifer code cannot be deleted, so parity drift between GPU noise-stage decisions and CPU carver-stage decisions on the same cells could create seams (e.g., carver places water where GPU decided stone). The two must consume the SAME FluidStatus source (either read back the GPU cell grid ~6KB/chunk, or keep computeFluid CPU-authoritative and upload it — the latter halves the port scope but keeps ~2-4pp of CPU cost).
- Beardifier-on-GPU adds per-chunk piece upload + junction handling; the ±12 cube-reach bug history shows this is easy to get subtly wrong. The hybrid mitigation (CPU-decide blocks inside the known beard AABB, ~0.8% of blocks) caps the risk but adds a dual-path branch in the consume loop.
- Blending chunks (world borders against pre-existing terrain) and any non-full extent must take the CPU path; on a fresh pregen that is ~0%, but in live play near old regions the GPU path silently degrades to today's 1.000x — the win is pregen/new-terrain-shaped.

## Lens 3 — Dead-end mechanics reconciliation: the residency -33% autopsy, the multi-worker fp64 corruption, and the relaxed-parity verify-gate design, evaluated against the COMPACT-READBACK candidate architecture (code-verified in src/main/java/dev/superchunk/gpu/** + kernels, plus the validated memory record)

### Findings
- MECHANISM M1 (per-chunk corner re-materialization) — root cause located in code: the full-field kernels are SINGLE-chunk-layout (they read the per-worker rt.outMem corner buffer, layout out[k*n+i], no origins table), so every batched chunk had to re-UPLOAD its host corners back to the device (GpuFusedInterpolator.provideFullFieldFromHostCorners L1128 -> rt.uploadCorners L1166, src/main/java/dev/superchunk/gpu/dfc/GpuFusedInterpolator.java) and dispatch 2 field kernels + 2 large readbacks PER CHUNK. Compact-readback avoids this ENTIRELY: the batch dispatcher's device buffer already holds K<=32 chunks' corners in out[(c*M+k)*n+i] layout with a K*3 origins table (OpenCLAstEmitter.emitMultiChunk L350-400; GpuBatchDispatcher.outMem L75), drained by ONE thread with a single sync point (batchFill L264-304). A multichunk decision kernel chained on that same in-order queue, between the corner kernel and before buffer reuse, consumes the corners with zero re-upload — the exact indexing pattern already exists in df_batch_lattice_multichunk.
- MECHANISMS M2-M5 all AVOIDED by construction, not by tuning: (M2 coalescing collapse 917x->165x) decisions run inside the same drainer dispatch so corner batching/coalescing is untouched (currently ~1099x with mwc768); (M3 launch explosion 13.4k->76.8k) compact adds +1-2 kernel launches per BATCH (up to 32 chunks), not 4-5 per chunk; (M4 blocking 5.25-10.5MB readback on the worker path) block-ids are 96KB/chunk (98,304 blocks x 1B) = 55-109x smaller, read back on the DRAINER like today's 78KB corners and delivered as data via GpuBatchStore; (M5 short begin->first-use window, ~5% hide) irrelevant because the batch path is a pipeline stage AHEAD of the noise status — GpuBatchPrefetch.wrapNoise runs at the biomes->noise boundary, frees the worker via the RxJava Completable (GpuBatchPrefetch.java L62-189), and the store already serves 100% before use (441/441, 16551/16551 in every measured run).
- The residency architecture's killer INTERMEDIATE need not exist in compact-readback: residency materialized M*98,304 doubles/chunk (5.25MB, x2 for lerp3 = 10.5MB) because the CPU consumed raw density; a device-side decision kernel interpolates per block IN-REGISTER (8 corner fetches + 7 lerps for finalDensity in vanilla X->Y->Z lerp3 order, plus the fluid/vein roots), reads back only the 1-byte verdict. A K=32 batched full-field buffer would be ~176MB; the in-register form needs none of it. OpenCLDevice.java holds exactly ONE CL command queue per device (out-of-order if supported, else in-order), shared via retain/borrow, with maxConcurrentTasksPerDevice=32 in-flight batches sequenced by event waitlists, event-callback completion, and 120s timeouts. Its only NVIDIA workarounds are compile-flag issues (incomplete CL3.0: '-cl-no-subgroup-ifp' rejected; no fast-compile pre-SM50); rusticl/Mesa needs explicit clFlush. But Config.useDevicePrioritization documents 'For stability reasons: Disable Nvidia GPUs if anything else is present on Linux' — acknowledged NVIDIA-Linux flakiness handled by avoidance. Meanwhile NVIDIA staff (Robert Crovella) assert cross-queue concurrent kernels with separate buffers are supported and reliable (https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/opencl-command-queue-overlap/232769). Conclusion: no public prior art matches our multi-queue fp64 corruption; the production-proven mitigation is single-queue-per-device serialization with large batched dispatches, which c2me demonstrates costs nothing at 1200–2500+ cps.
- BATCHING MODEL: spatially ALIGNED 4x4-chunk tiles (BATCH_SIZE=4; 2x2 via useSmallerBatches for iGPU TDR avoidance), fusing the BIOMES and NOISE chunk statuses into one enqueue chain per tile: 1 rwData upload (includes structure/beardifier data + Stage1 surface heights) -> df_biome_multinoise_kernel (16x16x96 quarts, uint32 biome index per cell, own async readback) -> df_interpolator_buffer_prefill_kernel (corner grid, cells+1 dims — same corner-grid idea as ours, but kept device-resident) -> aquifer_data_prefill -> df_cache2d_prefill_kernel -> df_noise_kernel -> block-id readback. Write-protect masks skip chunks already past status. This is temporal-window-free deterministic spatial batching: the GPU computes 16 chunks the moment the first aligned tile is schedulable — 'GPU ahead of CPU' by construction, with Stage1 16x16-chunk tiles running even further ahead.

### Key numbers
- c2me-ocl block-id readback: 1 byte/block host readback; 64x64x384 blocks = 1,572,864 B = 1.5 MiB per 4x4-chunk batch = 96 KiB/chunk — EXACTLY our compact-readback estimate, and 55–110x smaller than our measured residency readback (5.25–10.5 MB/chunk). (Device-side blockOut buffer is allocated with 4x slack, horizontalSize*verticalSize*horizontalSize*4 B, but only 1 B/block is read back.)
- Biome readback: uint32 per quart cell, 16x16x96 quarts = 98,304 B per 16-chunk batch = 6 KiB/chunk.
- Dispatch density: 1 upload + 4–5 kernels + 2 readbacks per 16 chunks ~= 0.5 enqueues/chunk, vs our corner path's ~1 fused dispatch/chunk (K<=32 temporal batching) and vs the 77k-launch explosion (from 13k) that drove our -33% residency loss. Spatial 4x4 tiling is their coalescing mechanism.
- Stage1 area tile: 16x16 chunks = 64x64 quart surface heights (int32) = 16 KiB/tile readback, amortized ~64 B/chunk; 256-tile Caffeine cache; per-chunk consumption window 36x36 quarts (chunk + 4-chunk halo). Aquifer prefill NDRange ~7x34x7 ~= 1.7k work items per 16-chunk batch (16x12x16 cell spacing, +/-5-block margin) — negligible GPU cost.
- Queue/concurrency discipline: exactly 1 CL command queue per device; maxConcurrentTasksPerDevice default 32; cl_khr_priority_hints/throttle_hints low-priority queues default ON; 120 s per-task timeout with CPU fallback; readback into UNPINNED memAlloc buffers with event callbacks (they don't bother with pinned memory at 96 KiB/chunk).
- Published perf targets (Modrinth, with Chunky): 1200+ cps on GTX 1060-class GPUs (fp64 ~0.13 TFLOPS!) with mid-range CPUs; 2500+ cps on GTX 1080 Ti/RTX 4060 Ti-class with flagship CPUs; '80+%' uplift when CPU-bound. Derivation for our box: RTX 3070 (~20.3 fp32 / ~0.32 fp64 TFLOPS) sits between their 1200 and 2500 GPU tiers and the GTX 1060 data point proves fp64 rate is not limiting (corroborates our fp64-vs-fp32-FLAT measurement); i7-14700K sits between their CPU tiers -> implied ceiling roughly 1200–2000+ cps vs our current 450–664 cps = ~2–4x headroom for the compact-readback architecture.
- NVIDIA fast-compilation flags (their default ON): '-20% GPU-bound throughput' per their own config comment — a measured NVIDIA compile-flag tradeoff we can also test.
- Batch sizing: BATCH_SIZE=4 (4x4 chunks) default, 2x2 with useSmallerBatches (older iGPUs, GPU-timeout avoidance, 'will reduce scheduling efficiency for modern GPUs'); Stage1 cache tile 16x16 chunks (8x8 smaller-batch mode).
- Addressable CPU work per our JFR if we replicate the architecture: ~29.7% DF eval + ~12.1% aquifer + ~8.8% biome + part of 11.9% palette writes ~= 55–60% of chunk-fill wall moved off CPU workers -> naive fill-stage speedup ~2.2x; consistent with their 80%+ end-to-end when other stages (features/structures, still CPU) are included.
- c2me's config logic also keys off chunky.maxWorkingCount (fallback 192 if unset) — independent corroboration of our +9% maxWorkingCount=768 lever family.
### Risks
- LICENSE: c2me-opts-accel-opencl files are 'All Rights Reserved / Do not redistribute' (2025-2026 ishland) even though publicly hosted. We may use the architectural facts (compact ids, tile batching, single queue, FP_CONTRACT OFF, surface-height area cache) but must clean-room all code and kernel text; no paraphrase-porting of their emitters or .cl source.
- Their headline numbers are for MC 26.x + Java 25 + their rewritten chunk system + native fast-copy + revamped DFC. Our 1.21.1 NeoForge merged stack (different chunk system, different DF set: 104 DFs) may not reach the same tiers; the 2–4x headroom estimate is a ceiling argument, not a promise.
- FP_CONTRACT OFF is a hypothesis for our 3e-8 spline drift, not yet a measurement — if the drift instead comes from transcendental libm differences (pow/exp) it won't fully close, and the aquifer gate reverts to the relaxed-parity/block-flip-quantification path. Also note our prior finding that kernel micro-changes measured HARMFUL — FP_CONTRACT OFF must be validated for throughput too (though latency-bound suggests it's free).
- Single-queue-per-device serialization removes the multi-queue overlap our current corner path uses; it only performs because dispatches are huge (16-chunk tiles). Adopting it piecemeal (single queue + our current per-chunk dispatches) could regress before the tiling lands — sequencing of the migration matters.
- 4x4 aligned spatial batching requires scheduler-level changes: aligned tile scheduling, BoundedRegionArray-style neighbor access, write-protect masks for already-generated chunks, and blending fallback (Blender/old-chunk upgrades go CPU). This is chunk-system surgery in our vendored C2ME, historically the riskiest area.
- Parity relaxations we inherit by construction: 1–2-block biome border shifts (order-dependence) and cell-granularity beardifier for datapacks referencing it directly (per-block beardifier would 'halve the gpu throughput' per ishland). Both are within the user's accepted envelope for vanilla worldgen but widen the compat surface for modded worldgen — c2me-ocl is 'known to crash on pretty much all non-vanilla worldgen' and blocklists Enderscape/Bumblezone/BoP/TerraBlender/Big Globe.
- NVIDIA-on-Linux stability: ishland deprioritizes NVIDIA GPUs on Linux 'for stability reasons' without a targeted workaround — our fp64 artifact may be one face of a broader NVIDIA-Linux driver flakiness that single-queue discipline reduces but does not provably eliminate; the 0.005–0.01% corruption experiment must be re-run under the new topology before shipping aquifer-on-GPU.
- GPU worldgen inherits driver-bug support burden ('worldgen bugs now include GPU driver bugs'): timeouts, rusticl flush quirks, world-corruption-on-init-failure reports (C2ME-fabric issue #568 closed not-planned) argue for our own robust CPU fallback and init hardening.
