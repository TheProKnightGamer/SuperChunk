STATIC AUDIT  (2026-10-29: SuperChunk's mixins against the top 989 NeoForge 1.21.1 mods)
=============================================================
  METHOD. The newest file of each of Modrinth's 1,000 most-downloaded NeoForge 1.21.1 mods (989
  had one), nested jars included, was read with ASM: every mixin config, every @Overwrite and
  injector, refmap-resolved. Each was matched against SuperChunk's own jar for the conflicts Mixin
  turns into a crash or a lost change:
    - a mod's injector other than HEAD/RETURN/TAIL in a method SuperChunk @Overwrites at the same
      or higher priority (Mixin refuses it even with require = 0; GitHub #27, CBC), and the reverse;
    - two @Redirects, or a @Redirect and another call-site injector, on one call;
    - two @Overwrites of one method (the higher priority wins, the other change is lost);
    - SuperChunk cancelling or wrapping a method before a mod's hook in it runs.
  Separately, every class's constant pool was searched for the ids and package names of the mods
  SuperChunk bundles (lithium, c2me, vmp, scalablelux, noisium and their forks): mods that adapt
  to one of those by mod id or class name cannot see the relocated copies. And every @Overwrite
  was compared with NeoForge's patched original for event hooks the overwrite drops. Standalone
  copies of the bundled mods were left out (SuperChunk stands its copy down or declares them
  incompatible).

FIXED  (was incompatible; SuperChunk changed to support it)
=============================================================
  (all worlds) .......... aquifer water at cave edges .......... one-block differences from vanilla

    C2ME's aquifer rewrite (bundled here; upstream C2ME has the same code) looked at the 12 nearby
    aquifer centres in a different order than vanilla: y, z, x instead of x, y, z. Vanilla keeps the
    later of two centres at the same distance, so the order decides which aquifer a block belongs to
    when two are equally near. That gave one-block shifts of water, air and stone at cave and aquifer
    edges, in about 0.01% of columns. Found by comparing Distant Horizons' terrain-only LODs with and
    without SuperChunk: 122 of 676 sections differed. Every copy now uses vanilla's order: the CPU
    mixin and its candidate memo, both loops in the GPU's aquifer.cl, and their Java reference
    (AquiferRef, AquiferKernelSelfTest). After: 0 of 676 differ, on the CPU and the GPU. Same fix on
    Forge 1.20.1, whose vanilla code has the same order.

  distanthorizons ....... DH region's biome memo ................ stale biomes

    The biome quart memo assumed a region's biomes do not change while the region is used. DH's
    region (a WorldGenRegion subclass) is kept for all of a group's steps while the group's biomes are
    filled, so a memoized answer could go stale: 33 verify mismatches in a DH pregen, and a few
    surface blocks (grass against snow) different from DH alone. The memo now applies to vanilla's
    region class, and to DH's with only answers from chunks that already have their biomes kept. Its
    uniform-cell shortcut reads a cell's 8 corners only when all of them are stored, since reading a
    corner of a chunk without biomes runs a climate search whose warm start decides later ties.

  modernfix ............. ModernFix ............................. chunk patches it keeps off for C2ME

    ModernFix turns off mixin.bugfix.chunk_deadlock, mixin.perf.release_protochunks,
    mixin.bugfix.paper_chunk_patches and mixin.perf.nbt_memory_usage when the c2me mod is installed:
    they patch the vanilla chunk pipeline (the full-chunk executor, proto-chunk release) that C2ME's
    chunk system replaces. It checks the mod id only, so with the bundled C2ME they stayed on (all
    four are on by default). compat/ModernFixCompat now turns the same options off: through a mod
    override on ModernFix's loaded config, or, when SuperChunk's mixin plugin loads before
    ModernFix's, the modernfix.config.<option> property ModernFix reads. An option the user set with
    that property is left alone. ModernFix's vmp check (an ingredients module) and lithium check are
    for code SuperChunk does not bundle. Kill switch: -Dsuperchunk.compat.modernfix=false.

  asyncparticles ........ AsyncParticles (client) .............. thread-safe entity map undone

    With its safe ClassInstanceMultiMap option on, AsyncParticles cancels Lithium's
    collections.entity_by_type mixins through a MixinSquared canceller, matching
    "net.caffeinemc.mods.lithium.mixin.collections.entity_by_type"; the bundled copy lives under
    dev.superchunk.* and applied anyway. compat/MixinSquaredBridge registers a canceller of its own
    when MixinSquared is installed: for a bundled mixin it asks the other cancellers about the
    original name (for Lithium also the me.jellysquid package) and cancels the copy if any would
    cancel the original. Works with MixinSquared 0.1 to 0.3. AsyncParticles was the only canceller
    naming a bundled mod in the 989 mods. Kill switch: -Dsuperchunk.compat.mixinSquared=false.

  copycats, create_connected .. Create: Copycats+, Create: Connected .. wrong path type on copycats

    Both make mobs path over a copycat block as over the block it copies, by wrapping getBlock() in
    vanilla's WalkNodeEvaluator lookup and in Lithium's PathNodeDefaults.getNodeType, which the
    mixin.ai.pathing cache answers from. The second mixin names the class by its old Lithium
    package, so it never applied to the bundled copy. mixin.ai.pathing is now off when either is
    installed, and vanilla's lookup runs with their wrap. Kill switch:
    -Dsuperchunk.compat.copycatPathing=false.

  (general) ............. ChunkTicketLevelUpdatedEvent ......... never posted

    NeoForge's patch of ChunkMap.updateChunkScheduling posts it when a chunk's ticket level
    changes; C2ME's overwrite of that method (from upstream C2ME, which is Fabric code) dropped it,
    so its listeners (CC: Tweaked's computer chunk tracking is one) never heard of a change. The
    overwrite now fires it as NeoForge's patch does, unless neither level is a loaded one. The Forge
    port already did. Found by comparing every @Overwrite with NeoForge's patched original; the
    chunk load/unload, watch and data events all still fire (VMP and C2ME call the patched methods).

  (general) ............. processUnloads hooks, dotted lithium:options .. Forge crash, GitHub #27

    Found through Valkyrien Skies on the Forge port; the same code is here. C2ME's processUnloads
    overwrite (priority 1100) blocked other mods' hooks inside it: Mixin refuses any injector other
    than HEAD/RETURN/TAIL into a method overwritten by a mixin of the same or higher priority, even one
    marked require = 0, and fails the whole class. It is now a @WrapOperation on its removeIf call,
    with the same unloading. And a mod's lithium:options written as a dotted TOML key
    (mixin.ai.poi = false) is now read, where it was dropped as a nested table.

  skein ................. Skein ................................. parallel tick phases

    Updated Skein exposes version 1 of its optional SuperChunk cell bridge. With both updated
    jars installed, dimensions, entities, random blocks, block entities and opted-in scheduled
    ticks can run in parallel. Older Skein builds retain dimension-only compatibility. Existing
    user settings still decide which supported phases run; scheduled ticks remain opt-in.

    Before Lithium selects its mixins, SuperChunk disables nine shared-cache options when Skein
    is installed: alloc.chunk_random, util.world_border_listener,
    world.block_entity_ticking.world_border, world.chunk_access, block.hopper,
    chunk.entity_class_groups, entity.inactive_navigations,
    entity.collisions.unpushable_cramming and util.block_entity_retrieval. This applies to bundled
    and standalone Lithium through the generated lithium.properties. Other optimizations remain
    available. Skein checks the actual startup configuration, including the relocated bundled
    implementation; editing the file after boot cannot pretend an applied mixin is disabled.

    Whole-level access requires the exact dimension owner inside its active barrier. Cell workers
    cannot claim that role. They may drive their level's chunk executor only while holding Skein's
    chunk-load lock; unrelated threads retain the original checks. Bundled chunk lifecycle
    callbacks use this rule, and updated Skein supplies the corresponding standalone Lithium hook.
    Random isolation must already exist on every level before cell phases are allowed.

    Command blocks and command minecarts defer whole transactions across cell and dimension
    barriers, including selectors, cross-dimension teleports, success counts and conditional
    chains. Configuration and registry refreshes wait until workers finish. These checks remain
    active when dimension threading is disabled but cell threading is enabled.

    SuperChunk owns chunk serialization. Skein parallelSaving stays disabled; whole-server save
    operations retain their server-thread requirement. C2ME's all-level autosave scan pauses
    during dimension work. The live compatibility policy is reapplied after config reloads.

    VALIDATION: 120 bridge ownership/configuration/command-queue checks. Real transformed-server
    tests exercised all four in-level phases together in three dimensions with bundled and
    standalone Lithium 0.15.4: 288 hopper/chest pairs conserved all items, 96 random-ticked leaves
    decayed, 192 scheduled leaves updated, and four concurrent cell tasks loaded missing chunks.
    The same run passed 32 seeded explosion comparisons, 512 concurrent projectile-query checks,
    and 100 entity stress ticks split between the cluster and grid schedulers.

    Packaged jars on NeoForge 21.1.248 passed 1,089 requested chunks of generation, saves/restart,
    tick-function and command-block reloads, inventory transfers in all dimensions, local and
    cross-dimension spreadplayers, conditional chains, and powered command-minecart commands.
    Reproduce that suite with tools/verify-skein-server.py --parallel-phases. Omit the flag when
    testing older, dimension-only Skein. This is bounded compatibility testing, not a general
    modpack guarantee.

  modernfix ............. ModernFix (singleplayer only) ........... PASS (after fix)

    Reported 2026-08-05: with ModernFix's mixin.perf.suspend_integrated_server_during_load (ON by
    default) chunks around spawn stayed permanently invisible in singleplayer. Dedicated-server
    boot testing never covered this — the option only exists for an integrated server.

    Root cause was SuperChunk-side, in the VMP player-watching port: it no-ops vanilla's
    ChunkMap.applyChunkTrackingView but did NOT port upstream VMP's matching redirect of the
    setChunkTrackingView(EMPTY) reset in updatePlayerStatus, so a joining player was left on
    ChunkTrackingView.EMPTY and every chunk that completed before the first ChunkMap.tick() was
    dropped by onChunkReadyToSend and never re-offered. ModernFix stretches that sub-tick window
    across the whole world load.

    FIX: ported the missing upstream redirect into MixinTACSCancelSending. Full mechanism and
    proof are in that method's javadoc (redirectChunkTrackingViewSet).

  twilightforest ........ Twilight Forest (4.8.3345) .............. PASS (after fix)

    Reported 2026-08-18: "Exception generating new chunk /
    java.lang.IllegalStateException: Requested chunk unavailable during world generation" as a chunk
    IO error report, on an IO-Worker thread, in a large pack containing Twilight Forest 4.8.3345.
    Requested chunk -2,-1 while generating -2,-2 (distance 1); generating status minecraft:full,
    dependencies [minecraft:spawn]; maximum allowed status null.

    Root cause was SuperChunk-side, in an upstream C2ME feature SuperChunk turns on by default. The
    MC-276863 workaround (chunkSystem.suppressGhostMushrooms) walks the post-processing list at
    CHUNK_SENDING and calls
    BlockState.canSurvive on every vanilla brown/red mushroom, deleting the ones that fail, using a
    WorldGenRegion built on ChunkPyramid.GENERATION_PYRAMID.getStepTo(ChunkStatus.FULL). That step's
    directDependencies() has a single entry, so the region's legal read radius is ZERO and
    WorldGenRegion.getChunk throws for anything outside the centre chunk. Upstream C2ME ships the
    option OFF; SuperChunk defaults it ON (it is the companion to player.sendAtChunkSending, which
    exposes the outer no-tick ring before post-processing), so this surface is ours.

    Vanilla MushroomBlock.canSurvive only reads pos.below(), always inside that radius — which is
    why the scan has never tripped before. Twilight Forest ASM-hooks that method
    (twilightforest.asmhooks.BlockHooks#modifySoilDecisionForMushroomBlockSurvivability). Read from
    the shipped 4.8.3345 bytecode, the hook is:

        if (!soilDecision.isDefault()) return soilDecision;
        for (int x = -1; x <= 1; x++)
          for (int z = -1; z <= 1; z++)
            if (!(x == 0 && z == 0))
              if (level.getBlockState(pos.offset(x, -1, z)).is(TFBlocks.TWILIGHT_PORTAL))
                return TriState.TRUE;

    Eight horizontal neighbour reads at y-1. For a mushroom on a chunk-border column that reaches
    exactly one chunk out — matching "Requested distance: 1" in the report. The throw escaped
    upgradeToThis and TheChunkSystem.handleTransactionException turned it into
    reportChunkLoadFailure + MARK_BROKEN — a cosmetic workaround killing the chunk.

    And the shape is not rare. Blocks.BROWN_MUSHROOM and RED_MUSHROOM both declare
    hasPostProcess(always), so EVERY worldgen-placed mushroom lands in the post-processing list and
    gets scanned; roughly a quarter of them sit on a border column. Any TF world hits this almost
    immediately.

    FIX: dev.superchunk.worldgen.GhostMushroomCheck. The canSurvive call is guarded, and any failure
    is treated as "it survives", i.e. the block is left alone. Deliberately the conservative
    direction: the scan exists only to DELETE blocks, so a check that could not be evaluated must
    never delete one. The guard is scoped to the single call, not the loop, so one bad position does
    not skip the rest of the chunk. Distinct (block, failure, culprit) signatures are logged once at
    WARN — naming the offending mod frame — capped at 16, the rest at DEBUG. Worst case is the
    pre-existing vanilla bug: a ghost mushroom near a chunk border for one post-processing round,
    which is exactly what upstream's default already accepts. Ported to forge-1.20.1 (where the
    equivalent region reads a 3x3 square, so the same hook needs a 2-chunk reach to trip it).

  (any mod) ............. broken chunk = server hang ............. FIXED (hardening)

    GitHub issue #7 ("Watchdog Crash"). The reported dump has the server thread parked in
    ServerChunkCache.getChunk while every c2me worker is idle on its semaphore — no work anywhere,
    yet the tick never ends and the watchdog kills the server after 60s with nothing in the report
    naming a cause.

    Root cause is SuperChunk-side and mod-agnostic. When a chunk upgrade throws,
    TheChunkSystem.handleTransactionException returns MARK_BROKEN and FlowSched sets FLAG_BROKEN.
    From then on StatusAdvancingScheduler.tickHandle0 simply RETURNED for that holder — it never
    completed the futures for the statuses the item can no longer reach, not even exceptionally
    (removal and downgrade do complete them, with UNLOADED_EXCEPTION; the broken path did not).
    Meanwhile chunkAbsent's redirected vanillaIf$getManagedLevel still reports the vanilla ticket
    level, so ServerChunkCache.getChunk walks straight into managedBlock(future::isDone) on a future
    nothing will ever complete. FLAG_BROKEN is cleared only when the item finally unloads, so a
    broken chunk held by a ticket stays broken and the park is permanent.

    Any mod that can make an upgrade throw turns into this hang. Already seen twice in the wild:
    Twilight Forest's mushroom canSurvive hook (see its entry above) and Structure Layout Optimizer
    via JigsawShapeTracker.seedTopLevel on an empty VoxelShape.

    FIX: ItemHolder.failPendingFuturesAbove — when the scheduler sees FLAG_BROKEN it fails every
    still-pending future above the current status with UNLOADED_EXCEPTION, which
    NewChunkHolderVanillaInterface maps to ChunkResult "Unloaded chunk". That is what vanilla does
    with a chunk that failed to load: a non-creating getChunk gets null, a creating one throws
    "Chunk not there when requested", and the preceding "Error upgrading chunk [x, z]" line in the
    log is the diagnosis. Not permanent — the flag clears on unload and the downgrade path in
    ItemHolder.setStatus replaces every completed future, so a later reload starts clean.

    Healthy in-flight chunks retain vanilla's unlimited wait. An optional diagnostic budget
    (-Dsuperchunk.chunkSystem.nonCreatingGetChunkBudgetMillis; default -1 = unlimited, 0 = no wait)
    drains main-thread tasks until it expires and then returns null. It is disabled by default:
    Lithium's ChunkAwareBlockCollisionSweeper is the create=false caller in the reported dump,
    and treats null as an empty chunk, so timing out a healthy but slow load can let entities fall
    through terrain. The broken-holder future completion above fixes the reported hang directly.

    retires after 60s idle (-Dsuperchunk.io.storageIdleRetireMillis, 0 disables); the next task
    transparently starts a fresh one, and close() starts one on purpose so the final flush still
    runs after a retirement. An abandoned storage now costs no thread at all, matching vanilla's
    shape. A one-shot WARN fires at 192 concurrent storages (-Dsuperchunk.io.storageLeakWarnAt)
    naming the situation, since the leak itself is upstream and worth reporting there.

  distanthorizons ....... Distant Horizons (LOD) .................. PASS + integrated (0.3.0)

    Added 2026-08-14. DH booted cleanly alongside SuperChunk from the start (see COMPATIBLE), but
    "boots" is not "uses": DH GENERATES WORLD CHUNKS of its own, and how much of SuperChunk it got
    depended entirely on which of its two generation routes it took.

    ROUTE 1 — distantGeneratorMode = INTERNAL_SERVER (DH's client-side default). DH asks the live
    server for each chunk: it adds its dh_server_gen_ticket at level 33 on the server thread, runs
    the distance-manager updates, then calls ChunkHolder.scheduleChunkGenerationTask(FULL, chunkMap).
    C2ME's chunk-system rewrite implements exactly that entry point
    (NewChunkHolderVanillaInterface.scheduleChunkGenerationTask -> getOrCreateFuture, raising the
    FlowSched target status), so these requests were ALREADY served by the whole SuperChunk pipeline,
    GPU offload included. Nothing to fix in the generation itself.
      What WAS wrong was DH's self-report. InternalServerGenerator.runValidation() looks up its own
    IC2meAccessor binding, which DH's loader creates only for a mod whose id is literally "c2me";
    SuperChunk is a C2ME merge under one id, so the lookup missed and DH printed — to the log AND to
    the player's chat — "C2ME missing, low CPU usage and slow world gen speeds expected". Exactly
    backwards. FIX: dev.superchunk.compat.DhC2meAccessor binds a reflective stand-in accessor
    (a java.lang.reflect.Proxy over DH's own interface — SuperChunk compiles against no DH type) into
    DH's ModAccessorInjector at server start, before DH's generator classes initialize. It reports
    "superchunk (bundled C2ME)" rather than impersonating c2me; nothing keys on that string. This
    changes no code path — DH's C2ME binding is a reporting flag — it only removes a false warning.

    ROUTE 2 — SURFACE / FEATURES modes (DH's own batch generator; FEATURES is the DEDICATED-SERVER
    default). Here DH never touches the server chunk system: BatchGenerationEnvironment drives the
    vanilla generation steps itself, on DH's own thread pool, one STEP at a time over a GROUP of
    chunks (StepBiomes.generateGroup -> createBiomes per chunk, StepNoise.generateGroup ->
    fillFromNoise per chunk). SuperChunk's CPU optimisations and its PER-CHUNK GPU density path do
    engage there (they hang off NoiseChunk, not off any scheduler), but the two CROSS-CHUNK GPU paths
    did NOT: both are wired into the chunk system's status delegate (VanillaWorldGenerationDelegate),
    which DH bypasses. The cost was not merely one dispatch per chunk instead of one per ~32 — the
    batched submit is also the ONLY carrier of the chained decide-kernel block ids (compact ids), so
    every DH chunk re-ran the whole CPU decide loop that server-generated chunks skip, and every DH
    chunk paid a funneled single-chunk climate dispatch at its biome stage.

    FIX: dev.superchunk.compat.DhWorldGenBridge + mixin.compat.MixinDhWorldGenStep
    (superchunk-dh.mixins.json, "required": false, gated by DhMixinPlugin on the DH mod id). The hook
    sits on AbstractWorldGenStep.getChunkWrappersToGenerate RETURN — one call per group, immediately
    before the loop, handing us DH's OWN filtered chunk list — and submits the whole group through
    the same submit/deposit code the chunk-system seam uses (GpuBatchPrefetch.submitForChunk /
    GpuClimatePrefetch.submitForChunk), then waits for the deposits. DH's per-chunk calls then consume
    them from GpuBatchStore/GpuClimateStore with no per-chunk dispatch — byte-identical consume path
    to a server-generated chunk. Blocking is correct here (unlike the chunk-system seam, which frees
    its worker): the waiting thread is DH's own, DH requires each step to finish synchronously
    (confirmFutureWasRunSynchronously), and one bounded wait covers the whole group.

    READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING A NUMBER. The GPU offload does NOT make DH's own generator faster — DH
    is not bottlenecked on density noise, and CPU-only is level with GPU+seam (490 vs 495). What the
    GPU did was COST DH ~20% (490 -> 390): its chunks paid per-chunk blocking dispatches and never
    received the batched block ids. The seam's honest value is removing that tax, worth +26% at 24
    threads (390 -> 495) and +34% at 14 (286 -> 383) against the shipped GPU-on default. SuperChunk's
    total win for DH is +33% over no-SuperChunk, and essentially ALL of it is the CPU-side merge.
    Do not expect the GPU to lift DH the way it lifts a Chunky pregen (+50%).

    WHY DH IS ~500 cps WHERE A CHUNKY PREGEN IS ~900-1250. Different engine, different shape:
      - DH's batcher fill is starved. Its generation groups are ~4 chunks and each DH thread BLOCKS
        on its own group, so the cross-chunk batcher achieves 4.4 chunks/dispatch (78% of dispatches
        <= 4 chunks) against 22.4 for a Chunky pregen ([17-32]=1967 of 2962 dispatches there). GPU
        time per chunk roughly doubles as a result. Raising gpu.batchWindowMicros to 3000 does NOT
        help (37 s / 466 cps) — it only adds latency to threads that are already blocked.
      - DH pays per-chunk work a pregen never does: createDhHeightMaps, DhLightingEngine
        .bakeChunkBlockLighting, LOD extraction + compression + SQLite writes, and neighbour access
        through DhLitWorldGenRegion's ReentrantLock.
      - Thread count. DH sizes its own pool (numberOfThreads); the chunk system runs 22 C2ME workers
        fed by Chunky's 3072 in-flight chunks. 14 -> 24 DH threads is +22% CPU-only / +28% GPU.
      - DH's steps run over a shrinking CUTOUT of each group (GetCutoutFrom /
        WORLD_GEN_CHUNK_BORDER_NEEDED_BY_GEN_STEP), so a group carries border chunks the later steps
        discard.
    None of this is fixable from SuperChunk's side without rewriting DH's generator; the density
    path it can accelerate is already fully served (0 misses).

    CAN THE BATCHES BE FORCED LARGER? Measured: no, not from either side.
      - gpu.batchWindowMicros 500 -> 3000: 37 s / 466 cps, WORSE than 500 (32-34 s / 489-500). DH's
        threads are BLOCKED while their group's dispatch is in flight, so window latency throttles
        the very arrival rate that would fill the batch. Batch size here is a symptom of blocked-
        thread concurrency, not a knob.
      - DH's group size is hardcoded, not configurable: core.generation.BatchGenerator returns a
        constant 0 from BOTH getSmallestDataDetailLevel() and getLargestDataDetailLevel(), and
        WorldGenerationQueue.canGenerateDetailLevel clamps every request to that range — DH always
        asks its own generator for the smallest region (~4 chunks/group). Confirmed empirically with
        enableNSizedGeneration=true: 4,076 noise groups for the same ~16.3k chunks (unchanged ~4
        chunks/group), 36 s / 454 cps.
      - The only real lever is DH's official override API
        (DhApiWorldGeneratorOverrideRegister.registerWorldGeneratorOverride): our own
        IDhApiWorldGenerator could advertise getLargestDataDetailLevel() > 0 and be handed large
        regions, returning EDhApiWorldGeneratorReturnType.VANILLA_CHUNKS. NOT PURSUED, and batch size
        is the wrong reason to pursue it: CPU-only already equals GPU+seam (490 vs 495), so cheaper

        pipeline, and note INTERNAL_SERVER — effectively that idea one chunk at a time — measured

DATAPACKS  (worldgen datapacks)
=============================================================
  Worldgen datapacks load as in vanilla; SuperChunk's shortcuts check what a datapack's density
  functions do before relying on them (the aquifer cell cache above; the GPU path gates every
  density function at registration). One vanilla limit behaves differently:

  jjthunder ............. JJThunder To The Max 0.9.0 .............. PASS (vanilla fails chunks)

    Its large_dripstone searches 512 blocks with wind drift and can read a chunk 9 away from the
    chunk being decorated; world generation allows 8. Vanilla throws ("Requested chunk unavailable
    during world generation") and the whole chunk fails, only once the terrain around it has been
    generated, so it shows up in explored worlds, not fresh pregens. Its neighbours then can never
    finish loading: vanilla hangs on them, and SuperChunk 0.4.0 before 2026-09-26 failed the load
    instead, which crashed the game when the player walked into one.
    SuperChunk now skips that one feature in that chunk and generates the chunk (one WARN per
    feature, then a count at most every 10 minutes; nothing changes for chunks vanilla can
    generate). The pack's far block writes, which vanilla drops and logs one line each (185,000
    lines in half an hour of play), are still dropped; the log gets one line per feature and a
    count. Replayed on copies of both affected worlds: every chunk that failed in play generated
    and saved as minecraft:full. Details: analysis/jjthunder-freeze-2026-09-25.md.
    Switches: -Dsuperchunk.worldgen.skipOutOfRegionFeatures=false (vanilla: fail the chunk),
    -Dsuperchunk.worldgen.logEveryFarWrite=true (vanilla's per-block lines).
    Pack-side fix: setting the pack's large_dripstone wind_speed to 0 keeps every probe within
    reach, and the feature then generates in full.

INCOMPATIBLE  (unfixable)
=============================================================
  betterchunkloading .... Better Chunk Loading .................... FAIL (hard, by design)

    Reported 2026-08-09: "no crash, the game just stalls and I have to kill it with task manager.
    It won't generate new worlds or already created worlds", in Create Chronicles: The Endventure.
    REPRODUCED 2026-08-13 by installing SuperChunk 0.3.0 + Better Caves + Streams Reflowing +
    Distant Horizons into that pack (321 mods, NeoForge 21.1.229, MC 1.21.1) and creating a new
    world. Here it surfaces as a CRASH rather than a silent stall:

      Description: Exception initializing level
      java.lang.NullPointerException: Cannot invoke "java.util.concurrent.CompletableFuture.isDone()"
          because "this.saveSync" is null
        at ChunkHolder.addSaveDependency(ChunkHolder.java:131)
        at ServerChunkCache.mdead87f$betterchunkloading$lambda$prioWaiting$1$0(...:1142)
        ... -> PlayerRespawnLogic.getOverworldRespawnPos -> MinecraftServer.setInitialSpawn
            -> createLevels -> loadLevel -> IntegratedServer.initServer

    MECHANISM. C2ME's chunk-system rewrite replaces the vanilla chunk pipeline, so vanilla's
    ChunkHolder future fields no longer mean anything under it. C2ME therefore deliberately
    BOOBY-TRAPS them: rewrites/chunksystem/mixin/MixinChunkHolder#failFastIncompatibility injects at
    ChunkHolder.<init> RETURN and nulls fullChunkFuture, tickingChunkFuture,
    entityTickingChunkFuture, pendingFullStateConfirmation and saveSync on every holder that is a
    NewChunkHolderVanillaInterface — i.e. on EVERY live holder, since that class extends ChunkHolder.
    The method name says the intent: a mod that reaches for vanilla chunk-holder state should fail
    loudly rather than quietly operate on state the chunk system no longer honours.

    Better Chunk Loading trips exactly that wire. Its ServerChunkCacheMixin hangs a whenComplete
    callback ("prioWaiting") on the chunk future and calls ChunkHolder.addSaveDependency from it;
    addSaveDependency reads this.saveSync.isDone() -> NPE. SuperChunk's FlowSched
    (ItemHolder.setStatus) is merely what completes the future, so it appears in the stack without
    being the defect. Because the trap fires at setInitialSpawn, a NEW world dies before it exists;
    an existing world hits the same call on any subsequent chunk load.

    NOT SUPERCHUNK-SPECIFIC. The tripwire is inherited VERBATIM from upstream C2ME
    (upstream/C2ME-neoforge/.../chunksystem/mixin/MixinChunkHolder.java:36-43; Yarn savingFuture ==
    Mojmap saveSync). Any C2ME build with c2me-rewrites-chunk-system active should fail identically.
    The reporter's "this doesn't happen with c2me and lithium installed" is therefore UNEXPLAINED —
    most likely that run had the chunk-system module disabled, or Better Chunk Loading absent.
    Worth confirming before anyone concludes SuperChunk is the differentiator.

    RESOLUTION: remove Better Chunk Loading. There is no SuperChunk-side fix — the two are competing
    implementations of the same subsystem, and the NPE is C2ME's intended failure mode, not an
    accident to patch. Nothing is lost: prioritised/faster chunk loading is what SuperChunk's chunk
    system already does.

    ENFORCED SINCE 0.3.0: neoforge.mods.toml declares betterchunkloading as
    type = "incompatible", so the loader now refuses to start and shows an error screen naming both
    mods plus a reason, instead of the mid-worldgen NPE whose stack names SuperChunk classes (which
    read as a SuperChunk bug and cost this investigation several rounds). NeoForge has no API for one
    mod to DISABLE another — refusing to launch is the strongest supported action, and the honest one
    here, since the combination cannot work at all.

    Bar for adding another entry to that list: a REPRODUCED failure with a known mechanism, written
    up here. Never list a mod on suspicion — an entry bricks the launch for everyone who has it, so a
    false positive is worse than the bug it guards against. Where the conflict is OURS to yield, stand
    our own feature down instead (StructureLayoutOptimizerCompat, ByePregenCompat, the bundled-Lithium
    stand-down); that option does not exist here, because the chunk system IS SuperChunk.

    SAME-RISK, UNTESTED, ALSO IN THAT PACK: chunksending (its own ChunkHolderMixin), smoothchunk,
    cupboard and sable all mix into ServerChunkCache/ChunkHolder. None got the chance to fail before
    Better Chunk Loading did — removing betterchunkloading may simply surface the next one.

  scalablelux / c2me / noisium ... the standalone copies of bundled mods ... FAIL (hard, by design)

    REPRODUCED 2026-09-25: a dedicated server with SuperChunk 0.4.0 plus ONE of ScalableLux 0.1.0.1,
    C2ME-NeoForge 0.4.0-alpha.0.106 or Noisium 2.7.0 (all for 1.21.1) exits within seconds, before
    the mod list:
      scalablelux, c2me: IllegalArgumentException: Decoration with key 'fabric-modId' already exists
        on config scalablelux.mixins.json / c2me-base.mixins.json. FML decorates every mixin config
        with the id of the mod that lists it, and both mods ship configs under the exact names
        SuperChunk registers for its bundled copies.
      noisium: InjectionError: Critical injection failure: Redirector
        noisium$populateNoiseWrapSetBlockStateOperation — its redirect collides with the bundled one.
    RESOLUTION: remove the standalone jar; SuperChunk already contains it. Declared incompatible in

    naming the mod to remove. Renaming our configs would NOT be a fix: the crash would become the

    reproduce with.)

  cbcmodernwarfare ...... CBC Neo Warfare 0.0.6v ................ NOT SUPPORTED, by choice
